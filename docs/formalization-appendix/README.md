It records the hypotheses, domain, semantic interfaces and limits of the formalized claims. It is an AI-assisted source correspondence prepared for expert verification, not a signed expert opinion, independent human certification, or journal peer review. It adds no mathematical theorem and revises no proof.

The normative mathematical statement is manuscript **Theorem 2.1**. The normative formal statements are the types and definitions in the pinned Lean source. A successful Lean check establishes the formal implication from those encoded premises. Agreement of those premises and conclusions with the physical interpretation and paper remains a task for the expert. In particular, a theorem name, source comment, finite numerical diagnostic or AI audit verdict is not a substitute for inspecting a type and its definitions.

## B. Theorem 2.1 and the actual optimization domains

Put $n=n_A+n_B$. For either statistic, the intended statement is existence of an admissible pure Gaussian density $\sigma$ with auxiliary counts $(n_A,n_B)$ such that

$$
S(\sigma_{AA'})=\inf_{m_A,m_B\in\mathbb N}\{S(\tau_{AA'}):\tau\text{ is an admissible pure Gaussian extension of }\rho_{AB}\},
$$

and $S(\sigma_{AA'})\le S(\tau_{AA'})$ for **each** feasible competitor at **each independently finite pair** $(m_A,m_B)$. The quantifier includes counts below, equal to and above $n$ in total, unequal side counts and zero auxiliary sides. It does not mean that every pair has a feasible purifier. No infinite auxiliary state or limiting infinite-mode competitor occurs.

Both roots finish with `infimum_eq_of_global_sidewise_witness` in their sector's `AllFiniteDomain.lean`; therefore the conclusion includes a feasible least-value witness, not merely an equality between two abstract infima.

There are two coordinate subtleties to check. First, bosonic coefficient vectors label observables, rather than operator columns. The paper's adapted structure is $J=-S^{-T}\Omega S^T$; an arbitrary frame requires simultaneous restriction of both $V$ and $\Omega$. The source Weyl convention is $W(q,p)f(x)=e^{ip(x+q/2)}f(x+q)$, with characteristic $e^{im(q,p)-V((q,p),(q,p))/4}$ and vacuum parameter one. `weylSymplecticForm` is $\langle q,p'\rangle-\langle p,q'\rangle$; [D16](DECLARATIONS.md#d16) writes uncertainty as $V(x,x)+V(y,y)-2\Omega(x,y)\ge0$. The sign is harmless because $y$ ranges over all vectors, but the coefficient ordering and normalizing factor must not be dropped.

Second, fermionic density restriction uses the graded CAR convention. `majorana` includes Jordan–Wigner parity strings; `sidewiseUnitary_implements` proves the selected whole-mode permutation has an actual implementation. Covariance is defined by the trace of the Majorana commutator, with convention $\Gamma_{ab}=-\frac{i}{2}\langle[c_a,c_b]\rangle$. A bare unsigned permutation of occupation basis entries does not establish the same restriction. See the existing [common-core physical-subsystem discussion](../COMMON_CORE.md#physical-subsystem-and-spectrum).

## D. Lemma 3.1: mode compression and exact equality rigidity

The paper chooses a complex line $P$ in $C\cap JC$ when $c>a$. It retains $U=P^{\perp}$ for fermions or $P^{\perp_\Omega}$ for bosons. This construction makes the **actual retained spectrum** a Hermitian compression. It does not assert that entropy decreases under every auxiliary deletion.

The paper orders complex-mode parameters increasingly. Lean's real self-adjoint eigenvalue list uses the opposite, descending ordering. Thus the bosonic upper interlacing comparison appears as `boson_prefix_comparison`, while the fermionic lower interlacing comparison appears as `fermion_suffix_comparison`. `realListCost` divides the real spectral sum by two because each complete Gaussian mode contributes a paired real eigenvalue. B09 and the fermionic adaptation bridges establish that pairing; dividing an arbitrary list by two is not a semantic proof.

Exact equality makes the trace of the positive discarded defect zero. Positivity of $K-I$ or $I-K$ forces the endpoint and decouples the selected factor. This supports the paper's equality clause **for its selected construction**. The existing [common-core note](../COMMON_CORE.md#exact-equality-and-global-deletion) already supplies counterexamples to arbitrary entropy-preserving deletion and explains why a small entropy gap does not authorize exact removal; those arguments need not be reproduced here.

For bosons, the paper's $\nu_{\max}\ge v_*/\sigma_{\min}(\Omega_R)$ is formalized through a compact-sublevel argument: continuity bounds the inverse covariance square root on the compact frame family; a finite entropy sublevel bounds the norm of the Hermitian pair operator; `bosonPair_inverse_norm` bounds the inverse restricted commutator; `matrix_inverse_bound_lower` excludes a singular limiting form. `exists_minimum_of_sublevel_lowerBound` then yields an admissible minimizer. B18 contains this chain and its exact hypotheses. This establishes the boundary-exclusion obligation without assuming a globally bounded squeezing parameter. A divergent frame describing a fixed cut is gauge freedom; the excluded escape is degeneration of the cut's restricted commutator.

For fermions the root path uses compactness of the actual finite-dimensional **density** feasible set, rather than depending on the paper's Grassmannian auxiliary-orbit proof. `StateTopology.lean` proves closedness of Wick constraints and purity and continuity of the genuine prefix trace, then compactness and continuous-objective attainment. F14 adds the finite minimum over all splits. This is a valid alternative implementation of the existence obligation; this appendix does not claim a full independent formalization of the paper's separately fixed-parity orbit statement. Consult the [architecture comparison](../PROOF_ARCHITECTURES.md) for the already documented mathematical alternatives; no new proof is introduced here.

Two generic declarations express the finite logic, with all sector-specific hypotheses visible:

- [G01: `Gaussian.Optimization.global_attained_from_optimal_deletion`](DECLARATIONS.md#g01) takes an indexed feasible family `E M` and real `cost`. It assumes attainment for every $M\ge n$, deletion **only at actual attained minima** at $M+1$, and padding from every feasible $M<n$ to $n$. These obligations explain the paper's plateau $e_M=e_{M-1}$ above $n$, the comparison with smaller feasible totals, and the final actual attainment. The frozen Lean roots use the sufficient nonincrease interfaces, rather than defining or asserting the entire numerical sequence $e_M$ as an extra premise. The bosonic density realization/comparison bridge B05/B06 and the fermionic actual-density construction complete the connection to the original infimum.
